## 1. Cleanups (beach/shore/sea, underwater, forest, plogging, World Cleanup Day, municipal, Princes' Islands)

### Takeaway
The most reliable large, social, open-to-the-public cleanup in Istanbul is TURMEPA's International Coastal Cleanup. It is held every year on the third Saturday of September; the 2026 edition was 19 Sept at Moda. The next one would fall on **18 Sept 2027** (an inference from that rule). Beyond that, cleanups are scattered and event-based: corporate and TURMEPA days at Caddebostan and Büyükada, foundation-run dive cleanups (Cemre Vakfı, Deniz Yaşamını Koruma Derneği), and municipal or ministry events. The only expat/English-friendly cleanup group found, Tidy Turkey, was well documented in 2018–2019, but no 2025–2026 activity could be confirmed.

### Inferences
- **Upcoming dates (calendar arithmetic, not announced):** International Coastal Cleanup and World Cleanup Day 2027 would both fall around **Sat 18 Sept 2027**. Other recurring hooks are World Environment Day (5 June) and World Oceans Day / Marmara Sea Day (8 June). Expect TURMEPA's Istanbul site to rotate among Moda, Caddebostan and Büyükada.
- TURMEPA events are large (100–400 people), half-day and free, and they mix corporate teams, schools and individuals. That makes them sociable but one-off. To meet the same people repeatedly, a smaller recurring group (a dive club, Tidy Turkey if still active, a district municipality volunteer team) is better.
- Underwater cleanups need a scuba certificate. They are a natural fit for someone who joins an Istanbul dive club (e.g. the clubs that partnered on Büyükada), and dive clubs are inherently social, recurring communities.
- Kuzey Ormanları Savunması is advocacy (protest marches, public meetings), not hands-on cleanup. It is still a recurring, social Turkish-speaking community for people who care about the forests.
- Language: TURMEPA, the municipalities, Cemre Vakfı and KOS are all Turkish-language. Tidy Turkey and Eco Istanbul are the only English-friendly cleanup groups found, but their current activity is unverified.

## 2. Tree planting, nature conservation, urban gardens, seed swaps, birding

### Takeaway
**TEMA Vakfı** is the main structured route into ongoing, neighbourhood-level environmental volunteering in Istanbul. It has an open call for "Aktif Gönüllüler" (gonullu@tema.org.tr), is Turkish-speaking and has a strong group culture. **Birding** gives the most distinctive recurring citizen-science activity: Bosphorus raptor counts at Çamlıca/Karlıtepe in autumn and spring, and Yelkouan shearwater counts that peak in February. The İstanbul Kuş Gözlem Topluluğu (IKGT) runs weekend trips. Urban gardens (Kuzguncuk, Yedikule) and seed swaps (Kadıköy's annual April Tohum Takas Şenliği) are social, but they offer few formal volunteer slots. Buğday's TaTuTa/WWOOF farm stays are a weekend or holiday option outside the city.

**Buğday Derneği / TaTuTa (WWOOF Türkiye) farm volunteering**
- TaTuTa, now run as WWOOF Türkiye/TaTuTa by Buğday Derneği, places volunteers on organic farms. Volunteers pick a farm on the website, contact it directly, work there and make a symbolic payment to the farm. It is open to anyone 18+ regardless of experience. There were 79 host farms in 2021. — [NTV](https://www.ntv.com.tr/ekonomi/ekolojik-tarim-gonullusu-olun,34p3Lz73xUKI8o8nqmIF3g); [Greenpeace TR blog](https://www.greenpeace.org/turkey/blog/tatilden-ote-bir-tecrube-ekolojik-ciftlikler/); [Listelist](https://listelist.com/turkiye-ekolojik-yasam-ciftligi/); [Buğday 2021–2024 activity report (PDF)](https://bugday.org/blog/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/2021-2024-Faaliyet-Raporu-27.04.202.pdf)
- An older listing gives Buğday's Istanbul office in Kuledibi (Beyoğlu); it needs confirming. — [Buğday "the bugday movement"](https://bugday.org/blog/the-bugday-movement)

### Inferences
- **Best fits for meeting people regularly:**
  - TEMA Aktif Gönüllü: neighbourhood teams, regular meetings, Turkish needed.
  - IKGT weekend trips and the raptor/shearwater counts: small, regular, citizen science. Birding is international, so English likely works with some counters, but this is an inference.
  - Validebağ Savunması: weekly meetings, Turkish.
- **Seasonal calendar (inference, not announced):**
  - Yelkouan counts: Dec–Feb, peaking in February.
  - Spring raptor migration over Sarıyer: roughly March–May.
  - Autumn raptor migration over Çamlıca: roughly late August–October.
  - Earth Hour 2027 at a WWF venue: late March. The 2024 edition was 23 March; I have not confirmed the 2027 date.
  - Kadıköy seed swap: likely an April 2027 Sunday.
  - Tree planting: traditionally 11 November (National Afforestation Day) and Forest Week, 21–26 March. This is general knowledge, not verified for Istanbul TEMA events in this research.
- TaTuTa farm stays suit weekends or holidays rather than city-based recurring volunteering. A TaTuTa farm within day-trip range of Istanbul (e.g. Şile, Kandıra, Sakarya or Yalova areas) would need to be checked on the site.

### Gaps
- No current (2025–2026) Istanbul TEMA event calendar was found, nor the TEMA Istanbul office address; a search summary gave Halaskargazi Cad., Şişli, but no source page confirmed it.
- No public-volunteer signup for Doğa Derneği in Istanbul, and no current organiser or contact for the Çamlıca/Karlıtepe raptor count.
- No volunteer or imece schedule for Kuzguncuk Bostanı, Yedikule or Kadıköy/Validebağ community gardens. "Ekoloji Kolektifi" was not found in results.
- Not researched (search budget ran out): zero-waste community groups, bicycle advocacy volunteering, OGM public planting days in Istanbul, Princes' Islands nature groups, and whether İKSV's bird programme involves volunteers.

## 3. Cultural and heritage volunteering (museums, ÇEKÜL, archaeology, İKSV festivals, cultural venues)

### Takeaway
Formal, open volunteer programmes at Istanbul's major cultural institutions are **thin or unadvertised**. No public volunteer programme was found for Istanbul Modern, Pera Museum, ÇEKÜL or İKSV in 2025–2026; Istanbul Modern and Pera offer paid "friends" or membership communities instead. The clearest documented heritage volunteer scheme is **Beykoz Kundura's** exhibition and oral-history guide programme, but the only document found dates from 2022. Archaeology, through Koç University's Küçükyalı ArkeoPark, has had community and volunteer elements in the past; no current public call was found. İKSV festival volunteering has existed historically, but no current call was found. The next big İKSV event is the 46th Istanbul Film Festival, **8–18 April 2027**.

### Inferences
- In Istanbul, people who want to belong to the art world usually join paid "friends" or young-member communities (Genç Modern, Friends of Pera) rather than volunteer. These are social and English-friendly, but they are memberships, not volunteering.
- Festival volunteering (İKSV, KuirFest, Leyli) tends to be seasonal, with open calls a few months before each event, and often targets students or under-30s. For the April 2027 Film Festival, any call would likely come in Jan–Mar 2027. This is inference only; no call has been confirmed.
- Excavation volunteering in Istanbul is effectively restricted to university-affiliated students and professionals, because digs run under Istanbul Archaeological Museums permits. An adult outsider would at most join community or education events at Küçükyalı ArkeoPark.
- Beykoz Kundura's oral-history guide scheme is the closest match to "heritage volunteering with a team". Its Asian-side location (Beykoz) is a commute consideration.

- No current public volunteer programme was found for İKSV, Istanbul Modern, Pera, SALT, Rahmi M. In culture, the only concrete volunteer form found is Beykoz Kundura's (2022, possibly outdated). Most Istanbul options require Turkish.

### Inferences
- **Sociability ranking (my judgement):**
  - High and recurring: TEMA neighbourhood teams, IKGT trips and count teams, dive clubs, Validebağ Savunması.
  - High but one-off: TURMEPA September cleanup, Kadıköy seed swap, Earth Hour.
  - Lower: TaTuTa (small farm households), Genç Modern (event attendance).
- **Asian vs European side:** many of the documented activities cluster on the Asian side (Kadıköy, Üsküdar, Maltepe, Beykoz, Tuzla). Belgrad Forest and Sarıyer, the cultural institutions in Beyoğlu, and İKSV are on the European side.
- **For a non-Turkish speaker:** birding (international community), WWOOF/TaTuTa (international network) and possibly Tidy Turkey, if it is still active, are the most English-accessible. Everything municipal, TEMA, TURMEPA and the activist groups will need at least basic Turkish.
