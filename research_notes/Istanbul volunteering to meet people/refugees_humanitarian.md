# In-person humanitarian volunteering in Istanbul (refugees/migrants, poverty, food, homelessness) — status as of Nov 2026

> Method note for the report writer: almost every page fetch failed in this session because the network blocked the hosts (smallprojectsistanbul.org, ahbap.org, ntv.com.tr, bianet.org, yabangee.com and gazeteoksijen.com all failed DNS). So the findings below come from search-engine result summaries and snippets of the cited pages, not from reading each page in full. Treat exact hours, addresses and sign-up mechanics as "verify before going". Where sources conflict, both are given.

## 1. Refugee/migrant organizations: who is active, what roles exist, language, commitment, checks

### Takeaway
Small Projects Istanbul (SPI) in Fatih/Çapa is still the clearest English-friendly, community-style option for helping refugees in person. Its own site still describes volunteer-taught Turkish classes, a women's craft collective and children's activities at its Olive Tree center, but I found no 2025–2026 news confirming current intake. Most other refugee NGOs (Mülteciler Derneği, SGDD-ASAM, Support to Life, Yuva, Caritas) are professional, staff-led organizations with no public walk-in volunteer program I could find. You would need to email them, and Turkish (with Arabic or Farsi a plus) is likely needed. Tarlabaşı Community Center, historically the best volunteer hub, has been shut down by the courts and should not be listed as an active option.

**Language and general advice**

### Inferences
- **Best fit for an English speaker who wants a community:** SPI. It has an expat-founded, expat-heavy team, volunteer-taught classes and weekly recurring sessions (after-school and weekend). Note one contradiction: its Turkish classes are "taught by trained volunteers", so teaching Turkish may need Turkish fluency, while craft, art, music and outdoor-play roles probably do not. This is an inference from the program list; no source states the language requirement.
- Mülteciler Derneği, SGDD-ASAM, Support to Life and Yuva look like professional, project-funded NGOs. Volunteering there is probably by request, skill-based (translation, psychosocial work, education) and in Turkish, with Arabic, Farsi or Pashto useful. UNHCR and AIDA both report 2025 funding cuts that shrank NGO programs (see section 4), which probably means fewer structured volunteer programs.
- Church groups (Union Church, Caritas) are the most likely English-speaking alternatives for in-person refugee aid such as clothing distribution and social programs for mothers. You would have to contact them directly.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 primary confirmation that SPI is still taking volunteers, and no figures on minimum commitment, background checks or orientation. Its site could not be fetched (DNS blocked in this session).
- No information found on Christ Church (Anglican), St. Antoine (Franciscan), "Hope for Istanbul", Refugee Rights Turkey (Mülteci Hakları Merkezi, mostly legal and professional) or specific "Kadın Dayanışma" refugee women's groups.
- No sources on background checks or child-safeguarding requirements at any Istanbul refugee NGO.
- I found no structured language-exchange or tutoring program for refugee children with a 2025–2026 call (other than SPI's described classes).

## 2. Food, poverty and homelessness organizations

### Takeaway
For social, recurring, group-based volunteering, the strongest active options are the Beyoğlu/Taksim homeless-feeding groups. Çorbada Tuzun Olsun hands out soup or food in the evening six days a week. Its volunteers are mostly university students and white-collar workers, and Erasmus/ESN groups regularly join in. Other options are Hayata Sarıl Lokantası (Beyoğlu) and Sokak Lambası Derneği (Fatih). Kızılay is the big structured channel: you apply online through its "Gönüllü Ol" platform, do some training first, and there are 4 Istanbul soup kitchens. TİDER (food bank / Destek Market) accepts volunteers but its Istanbul volunteer numbers are small. Ahbap, which used to be the most social volunteer network, has since July 2026 been under a criminal investigation, with its founder jailed and a state trustee (TMSF) running it. Do not recommend it.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Most social and easiest to join for meeting people:** Çorbada Tuzun Olsun. It runs nightly, it is a team activity (cooking, packing, distribution in groups), its volunteers are young and mixed students and professionals, it is central (Taksim), and international student groups already join it. That suggests mixed Turkish/English is workable for distribution shifts, but this is an inference; no source states its language policy.
- Kızılay is the most structured. The required online training before deployment means a slower start, but it gives recurring, team-based roles (soup-kitchen delivery, blood drives, disaster readiness) all over Istanbul. The platform and trainings are in Turkish, so Turkish is effectively required (inferred).

## 4. Practical cautions (closures, investigations, legal status of foreign volunteers, funding)

### Takeaway
Civil society in Istanbul is under real legal pressure. The best-known volunteer hubs have been hit: Tarlabaşı Community Center was dissolved by an appellate court in 2026, and Ahbap was put under a state trustee in July 2026 with a dissolution suit filed. Refugee NGOs also cut programs in 2025 because of funding losses. For foreigners, Turkish law requires a work permit or exemption before "working", and volunteering is not clearly defined in law. So someone on a residence permit should stick to informal, unpaid, ad hoc help with established associations and avoid anything resembling a regular job or role.

### Cited Findings
- Tarlabaşı Community Center: sealed in Oct 2024 ([Evrensel](https://www.evrensel.net/haber/530386/tarlabasi-toplum-merkezi-muhurlendi)). An appellate court ruling in 2026 declared the association non-existent or self-terminated, and the case went to Yargıtay ([FIDH](https://www.fidh.org/en/region/europe-central-asia/turkey/turkey-appellate-court-ruling-against-tarlabasi-community-center); [bianet](https://bianet.org/haber/tarlabasi-toplum-merkezi-hala-kapanmadik-dayanismaya-devam-ediyoruz-320523)). Amnesty International called the proceedings "judicial harassment" — [bianet / Amnesty](https://bianet.org/haber/tarlabasi-toplumunu-destekleme-dernegine-yonelik-yargisal-tacizlere-son-verilmeli-311959). Earlier coverage also reported that TTM volunteers received threats — [bianet](https://bianet.org/haber/bir-avuc-platformu-uyeleri-ttm-gonullulerini-tehdit-etti-290255)
- Ahbap: TMSF trustee, founder jailed, dissolution suit (see section 2) — [Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/trustee-takes-over-ahbap-charity-amid-misconduct-probe-225347); [Turkish Minute](https://turkishminute.com/2026/07/16/turkish-court-jails-rock-star-haluk-levent-13-others-in-ahbap-charity-investigation/amp/)
- In 2025 a sharp drop in humanitarian funding forced UNHCR and its partners in Türkiye to "drastically scale down or sequence community-based protection, legal counselling, outreach and other activities" — [UNHCR Annual Results Report 2025 Türkiye](https://www.unhcr.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/unhcr-annual-results-report-2025-turkiye.pdf)
- AIDA's 2025 update says many NGOs in Türkiye scaled down because of PRM and USAID funding cuts, which reduced refugees' access to services — [AIDA / ECRE 2025 update](https://ecre.org/aida-country-report-on-turkiye-update-on-2025/); [AIDA overview](https://asylumineurope.org/reports/country/turkiye/overview-main-changes-previous-report-update/)
- Earlier crackdown on international NGOs (detentions, expulsion of Mercy Corps). This is background and older — [Devex](https://www.devex.com/news/massive-anxiety-as-turkey-cracks-down-on-international-ngos-91004)
- Under International Labour Law No. 6735, a foreigner must obtain a work permit or work permit exemption *before starting to work* in Türkiye — [6735 sayılı Uluslararası İşgücü Kanunu (ÇSGB)](https://live.csgb.gov.tr/Media/lcohhstd/6735-sayılı-uluslararası-işgücü-kanunu.pdf); [ÇSGB FAQ](https://csgb.gov.tr/tr/sikca-sorulan-sorular/uluslararasi-ısgucu-genel-mudurlugu/calisma-ızni/)
- An academic article notes that volunteering is not explicitly defined in Turkish legislation, which leaves it unclear how foreign volunteers are classified — [DergiPark](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/4772595)
- A cloud-session constraint, not an Istanbul one: many primary pages could not be opened here, so statuses rely on search summaries.

### Inferences
- A foreign resident on a short-term or family residence permit is probably low-risk doing occasional, unpaid evening soup distribution or charity events with a registered association. Regular, timetabled roles (such as teaching weekly classes) are closer to "work". This is a grey area with no clear official guidance found, so suggest asking the organization how it handles foreign volunteers, and checking with Göç İdaresi or ÇSGB if unsure.
- Refugee-focused grassroots groups (especially rights-based or politically visible ones) face more legal risk than charity-style food or homeless groups and the state-aligned Kızılay. For a newcomer who mainly wants a social and good-cause activity, the lower-risk choices are SPI, Kızılay, TİDER, the church groups and Çorbada Tuzun Olsun.
- Because Ahbap and TTM were the two most "social" volunteer communities, readers who find old blog posts recommending them should be told that this information is out of date.

### Gaps
- I found no official Turkish guidance (Göç İdaresi or ÇSGB) specifically on unpaid volunteering by foreigners holding residence permits.
- I found no list of refugee NGOs closed or deregistered in Istanbul specifically in 2025–2026, beyond TTM and Ahbap (which is not refugee-specific).
- Whether Ahbap's local Istanbul volunteer groups still run any activities under the trustee is unknown.
