# In-person volunteering in Istanbul with children/youth/education, elderly people, people with disabilities and patients (as of late 2026)

> Research method note for the report writer: This was done on 2026-11-21. Direct page fetching was blocked in this research environment for tegv.org, losev.org.tr and sondakika.com. After that, all findings came from web-search result content that quoted or summarised the cited pages. The shared web-search budget ran out before these could be researched: Çocuk Vakfı, Yanındayız Derneği, Engelsiz Yaşam, sign-language groups, hospital clowning groups, "Abla-Abi" mentoring, intergenerational programs, and Türkiye Alzheimer Vakfı (a different body from the Derneği). They are listed under Gaps. Many organisations have no dated 2025-2026 call for volunteers. Each entry is marked where information may be outdated.

## 1. Children, youth, education and child patients: which organisations take adult (non-student) volunteers in person, and how?

### Takeaway
The best fits for an adult who wants recurring, group-based, social volunteering with children are TEGV (activity volunteer at an Istanbul learning unit or education park; 18+, free, online plus one-day live training), KAÇUV (child-activity volunteering in pediatric oncology wards and family houses; mandatory in-person intro meeting in Istanbul), LÖSEV (open sign-up, intro meetings every 15 days, active volunteer socials, choir and dance events in Istanbul) and Tarlabaşı Toplum Merkezi (the most English-friendly; 18+, interview plus orientation). AÇEV, Habitat Derneği and Genç Başarı need a bigger time investment as trainer or mentor, and recruit in seasonal calls. TOG is built around university students and youth, so it is a poor fit for non-students.

### Cited Findings

- Training follows the "Adım Adım Gönüllülük Modeli": foundation intro, then basic volunteer training, then program-specific training. Basic training is video content on the TEGV Dijital platform followed by a **1-day live training**. Volunteering is **free**. Apply at **www.tegv.org/gonullu-ol**. — [Ayancık Gazetesi](https://www.ayancikgazetesi.com/tegv-gonullu-basvurularini-baslatti-nitelikli-egitim-icin-sosyal-katki-cagrisi/94741); [Turkchem](https://turkchem.net/index.php/tegvde-gonullu-basvurulari-basladi-gonullu-ol-gelecekte-izin-olsun)
- Roles: activity volunteer (etkinlik gönüllüsü), support volunteer, trainer volunteer and corporate volunteer. Activity volunteers run programs for children face-to-face or remotely, in IT, maths, science, reading, art and English. On the application you pick your nearest activity point, and in-person volunteering at a point is emphasised. — [Bilim Şenliği](https://www.bilimsenligi.com/tegv-gonullulerini-bekliyor.html/); [Ayancık Gazetesi](https://www.ayancikgazetesi.com/tegv-gonullu-basvurularini-baslatti-nitelikli-egitim-icin-sosyal-katki-cagrisi/94741)

- TEGV has an English "Become a volunteer" page. — [TEGV EN](https://tegv.org/en/become-a-volunteer)
- Istanbul sites: the education parks Nusret Arsel, Sema ve Aydın Doğan, and Ferit Aysan are in Istanbul. A **2016** report lists the Istanbul learning units as Beykoz, İpek Kıraç, Semiha Şakir, Yeniköy and Zeyrek, so this list may be outdated. - Note: TEGV job ads in Ataşehir (e.g., corporate communications assistant) are paid positions, not volunteering. — [Youthall](https://www.youthall.com/tr/esassosyal/turkiye-egitim-gonulluleri-vakfi-tegv-kurumsal-iletisim-asistani-istanbul-hibrit_101)

### Inferences
- For "meeting people", **TEGV, LÖSEV and KAÇUV** look strongest. They have cohort-style onboarding (TEGV's live training day, LÖSEV's intro meetings every 15 days, KAÇUV's mandatory in-person meeting in Istanbul), recurring weekly activities and visible volunteer communities (LÖSEV's choir, dance events and Volunteer Day galas).
- **Turkish is effectively required** for TEGV, AÇEV, KAÇUV, LÖSEV, Koruncuk and ÇYDD, because the work is direct contact with Turkish-speaking children and families and all onboarding material is in Turkish. TEGV's English activity is a possible niche for English speakers. This is inferred from program content, not stated as a language policy.
- **TTM** is the most English-friendly in-person option for children, given Yabangee's recommendation and the English Group, but its application windows are irregular.
- AÇEV, Habitat and Genç Başarı are **trainer or mentor roles with seasonal calls**. They suit skilled professionals but involve a training investment (5 days to 2 weeks for AÇEV) and are less "drop-in social".
- TOG is structurally a **university-student and youth (17–25)** organisation, so a non-student adult will likely find limited entry points, apart from ad-hoc disaster volunteering.
- Expect **background checks and child-protection training** at child-facing organisations (TEGV's criminal-record consent, Koruncuk's mandatory training, TTM's child-safety policy).

## 2. Elderly: Darüşşafaka, Darülaceze, municipal nursing homes, Alzheimer organisations, intergenerational programs

### Takeaway
The clearest recurring route is **Darülaceze Başkanlığı** (Okmeydanı/Şişli, European side). It is a state institution, not İBB. It has an official volunteer form, takes anyone 18+, and its areas include reading and conversation, music, handicrafts and theatre, cognitive-support activities and workshop help, all under social-worker guidance. **İBB's own Darülaceze** (Kayışdağı campus, Asian side) is a separate institution with an older paper-form route. Its youth program (Genç Gönüllü) runs nursing-home activity sessions but only for **ages 15–25**. The Türkiye Alzheimer Derneği runs free day-care homes in Şişli and with Kadıköy Municipality, but no formal volunteer intake was found. One-off nursing-home visits appear on volunteer platforms (Gönüllüyüz BİZ, gonulluol.org), usually organised by youth groups.

### Cited Findings

## 3. Disability: TOFD, Down Sendromu, Tohum Otizm, Spastik Çocuklar, audiobooks for the blind, Special Olympics, guide running/cycling

### Takeaway
The most social, recurring disability option found is **Eşpedal**: sighted volunteer "pilots" ride tandem bikes with visually impaired co-pilots, pilots are trained, and it has an Istanbul representative. **Special Olympics Türkiye** (HQ Levent) uses volunteers and "partner athletes" at Istanbul events, e.g., 50 volunteers at the Dec 2025 Istanbul Games in Silivri. **TOFD** (Bakırköy) takes volunteers through an online form and has a social volunteer scene. Audiobook recording (GETEM, Kadıköy Sesli Kütüphane) is meaningful but mostly done **at home, alone**, so it is poor for meeting people. Tohum Otizm, Down Sendromu Derneği and Spastik Çocuklar Vakfı mostly take volunteers as **charity runners** (a social option via Adım Adım group training) rather than in hands-on roles. No organised guide-runner program for blind runners was found in Istanbul.

### Inferences
- For a person who wants to meet people while helping disabled people, the strongest options are **Eşpedal** (if they cycle), **Special Olympics events** (unified sports partner or event volunteer, especially around the December Istanbul Games) and **TOFD**.
- Audiobook reading suits someone with a good Turkish reading voice (or English, if GETEM records English titles; not confirmed). It is solitary and remote, so it fails the "meet people" criterion.
- For Tohum, Down Sendromu Derneği and TSÇV, the main public volunteer route is **charity running** via Adım Adım / İyilik Peşinde Koş. That is social through group training (see section 4), but it is fundraising, not direct contact with disabled people.
- **Turkish is needed** for virtually all hands-on disability roles. English helps mainly with Down Sendromu Derneği translation (remote) and possibly GETEM English recordings (unconfirmed).

## 4. Cross-cutting: sign-up platforms, language, age limits, cost, and how social each option is

### Takeaway
Nearly every option here is **free** and **Turkish-language**. Age limits matter: the minimum is 18+ (TEGV, Darülaceze, TTM), and some programs are youth-only (İBB Genç Gönüllü 15–25; TOG built around 17–25 and university students). Several aggregator platforms and charity-running groups are useful ways to find one-off social volunteering and to make friends.
